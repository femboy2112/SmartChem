## The structural reframe (load-bearing — it changes what "undercut" can even mean today)

Per the **USGS 2018 Minerals Yearbook — Bromine (Table 5, World Review)**, *every* producer worldwide extracts
bromine from **brine / seawater / Dead-Sea bitterns**: US (Smackover well brine), Israel/ICL (Dead Sea), Jordan/JBC
(Dead Sea), China (Shandong underground brine), Japan/Tosoh (seawater), India (seawater bitterns), Ukraine/Perekop
(underground brine). **There is no 2020s "mined bromine" reference to undercut.** The classic Dow comparison
("cheap brine route undercuts the mineral/cartel route") has no modern mined counterpart. The only meaningful modern
analog is **brine-route cost vs. prevailing market price** — a different question from the historical one, and one
the repo's R31 Theorem 1 ("modern degeneracy — no undercut demonstrable from same-benchmark data") already answers
in the negative on price data alone. This reframe corroborates that conclusion structurally.

## The candidate independent source: Gulf Resources, Inc. (China, Shandong underground brine)

- **What:** SEC-filed (CIK 885462), REALIZED, fully-independent-of-Albemarle cost-of-sales per tonne of bromine.
- **Figures (as summarized in GlobeNewswire press releases of Gulf's 10-Q/10-K — SECONDARY, pending primary check):**
  - FY2024: cost **$6,111/t ($6.11/kg)** at **8% plant utilization**; realized price ≈ RMB 17,561/t (≈ $2.47/kg,
    *my conversion at ~7.1 RMB/USD*); net loss.
  - Q3 2022 (least-distorted, 34% utilization): cost **$2,772.60/t ($2.77/kg)**; realized price ≈ RMB 51,795/t
    (≈ $7.3/kg); Q3 profit.
  - Q3 2023: $3,954/t (19% util); Q3 2024: $5,709/t (8% util).
  - Provenance: GlobeNewswire, "Gulf Resources Provides Detailed Overview of the Economics of its Bromine Segment"
    (2024-11-20) and "...Business Update on Bromine Segment" (2025-04-22), summarizing SEC 10-Q/10-K (CIK 885462).
- **Independence:** FULL — a Chinese underground-brine producer, unrelated to Albemarle or the Smackover/Magnolia
  forecast family.
- **Decisive caveat:** the cost/tonne is a *fully-absorbed accounting cost of sales* dominated by fixed-overhead
  under-absorption at pathologically low utilization (8–34%, from Chinese brine-well rectification shutdowns) — Gulf
  itself attributes the inflation to fixed costs spread over a small base. So it is a **loose UPPER bound**, not a
  steady-state cash cost, and the fixed/variable split needed to extract marginal cost is not disclosed. Gulf is also
  a distressed micro-cap (going-concern history). SEC-filed, but not a representative healthy producer.

**What it buys us:** at its least-distorted point (Q3 2022, 34% util) the *independent, realized* fully-absorbed
cost **$2.77/kg** brackets the R35 Albemarle/Magnolia forecast **$2.5365/kg** from a completely unrelated producer —
a genuine second bearing on the order of magnitude, and (being inflated by under-utilization) it implies a
full-utilization cash cost plausibly **below $2/kg**. This corroborates the forecast's magnitude *without* deriving
from it.

## Can a modern undercut be demonstrated?

- **Classic "brine vs. mined" form: NO** — there is no 2020s mined reference (all bromine is brine/seawater/Dead-Sea).
- **Modern analog (brine-route cost below prevailing price): regime-dependent.** Favorable regime (Gulf Q3 2022):
  realized cost $2.77/kg vs. price ~$7.3/kg → the brine route sat well below market (an independent "undercut").
  Depressed regime (2024): cost $6.11/kg (idle) vs. price $2.47/kg → losses, no undercut. This matches the repo's
  existing R31 conclusion (modern degeneracy at today's depressed single-benchmark prices) and adds an independent
  bearing on the *capacity* for a healthy brine route to sit below price in normal regimes.

Four filings pulled raw (HTTP 200) and sha256-anchored:

**The Q3-2022 headline is not stated verbatim in the filing — but it is exactly reconstructible from primary inputs,
using Gulf's own stated method.** Byte-verified quotes from the Q3-2022 10-Q:

- *"the net revenue for the bromine segment was **$19,845,773** and $14,913,004, respectively"* (3-mo ended 2022-09-30)
- *"the bromine segment had a net cost of revenue of **$7,362,103**"*
- *"The average selling price of bromine … was **$7,474**"* (3-mo ended 2022-09-30) — and the Q3-2023 10-Q confirms the
  method: *"$7,474 **(calculated by dividing revenue by tonnes)**"*
- *"the gross profit margin for our bromine segment was **63%**"*
- *"Our utilization ratio was **34%** for the three-month period ended September 30, 2022"*
- *"Utilization ratio is calculated based on the annualized actual production volume … divided by the annual production
  capacity … of all the seven factories **including those that have not commenced operations**"*
- *"Cost of net revenue reflects mainly the raw materials consumed … electricity, **depreciation and amortization** of
  manufacturing plant and machinery and other manufacturing costs"*

**Derivation:** tonnes sold = $19,845,773 ÷ $7,474/t = **2,655.3 t**; cost/tonne = $7,362,103 ÷ 2,655.3 t =
**$2,772.60/t = $2.7726/kg** (margin cross-check (rev−cost)/rev = **62.90%**, filing states 63%). This is a **$0.00
delta** vs. the press-release $2,772.60/t. The realized price the body recorded as *"≈ RMB 51,795/t ≈ $7.3/kg (my
conversion)"* is superseded by the filing's **direct $7,474/t = $7.474/kg**.

Degraded quarters, same derivation from primary inputs (corroborating the trajectory, not promoted): **Q3-2023**
$3,954/t (price $3,237/t, 22% gross loss); **Q3-2024** $5,785/t (price $2,396/t, 141% gross loss; the body's $5,709/t
is superseded by this primary derivation); **FY2024** 147% gross loss (directional — the annual MD&A does not restate
price for a clean per-tonne).

**Boundary (sharpened + primary-confirmed):** $2,772.60/t is a **fully-absorbed accounting cost** (the filing
explicitly folds in D&A), **not a cash cost**, at a 34% utilization that is *itself diluted* by non-commenced factories.
It is a **loose UPPER bound** on steady-state cash cost; the fixed/variable split needed for a marginal cash cost is
**not disclosed**. Gulf is a distressed micro-cap (going-concern history; the FY2024 10-K was amended twice in 2026).
SEC-filed and fully independent, but not a representative healthy producer.
